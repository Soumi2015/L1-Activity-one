OUR SOLAR SYSTEM

Our Solar System consists of the Sun and everything bound to it by gravity—including the eight planets, dwarf planets, moons, asteroids, and comets. Formed about 4.6 billion years ago from a collapsing cloud of gas and dust, it is located in the Milky Way galaxy's Orion Arm.

Introduction to the Planets:

PLANET MERCURY

Mercury is the smallest and closest planet to the Sun in our solar system. It is a rocky, terrestrial world with a massive metallic core. Because it lacks a substantial atmosphere to trap heat, it experiences extreme, life-incompatible temperature swings, ranging from \(430^{\circ}\text{C}\) during the day to \(-180^{\circ}\text{C}\) at night.

PLANET VENUS

Venus is the second planet from the Sun and Earth's closest planetary neighbor. Often called Earth's "twin" due to its similar size and mass, it is a scorching, hostile world. Its thick, toxic atmosphere traps heat in a runaway greenhouse effect, making it the hottest planet in our solar system.

PLANET EARTH

Earth is the third planet from the Sun and the only astronomical object known to harbor life. Formed approximately 4.5 billion years ago, it features a unique "Goldilocks" environment where liquid water covers about 70% of the surface, making it the perfect condition for ecosystems to thrive.

PLANET MARS

Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun and Earth’s closest planetary neighbor. Known as the "Red Planet," it is a cold, dry, rocky world with a very thin atmosphere. Its defining features include a rusty-red surface, two moons, and the largest volcano in the solar system.

PLANET JUPITER

Jupiter is the fifth planet from the Sun and the largest in our solar system. As a gas giant, it is primarily made of hydrogen and helium—the same materials as a star. It boasts a massive gravitational influence, numerous moons, and the iconic Great Red Spot storm.

PLANET SATURN

Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun and the second-largest in our solar system, famous for its dazzling, complex ring system. As a massive gas giant, it is composed primarily of hydrogen and helium, and is so light that it would actually float in a giant bathtub of water.

PLANET URANUS

Uranus is the seventh planet from the Sun and the solar system's third-largest by diameter. Classified as an "ice giant", it is famous for its unique ~98-degree axial tilt that makes it spin on its side, and its remarkably cold atmosphere.

PLANET NEPTUNE

Neptune is the eighth and most distant planet from the Sun. It is a massive, frozen "ice giant" known for its deep blue color, supersonic wind speeds reaching up to 1,200 mph, and being the first planet discovered entirely through mathematical prediction.

All ABOUT DWARF PLANETS

CERES

Ceres is the largest object in the main asteroid belt and the only dwarf planet in the inner solar system. Discovered in 1801, it is roughly 940 kilometers in diameter and holds about one-third of the asteroid belt's total mass. It is unique for its high water-ice content and potential past habitability.

MAKEMAKE

Makemake is an icy dwarf planet in the Kuiper Belt beyond Neptune. It is roughly two-thirds the size of Pluto, takes about 305 Earth years to orbit the Sun, and is notable for its reddish color and a faint methane atmosphere.

PLUTO

Pluto is a dwarf planet located in the Kuiper Belt, a distant region of icy bodies beyond Neptune. Discovered in 1930, it was famously classified as our solar system's ninth planet until 2006, when it was reclassified because it is not massive enough to clear its orbital neighborhood of other debris.

HAUMEA

Haumea is a uniquely egg-shaped dwarf planet in the Kuiper Belt, located beyond Neptune. Best known for its extreme spin, this oblong world rotates on its axis in just four hours—one of the fastest rotation rates in the solar system.

ERIS

Discovered in 2005, Eris is the second-largest and most massive known dwarf planet in our solar system. Located far beyond Neptune, its massive size initially triggered a scientific debate that led to Pluto's reclassification.
