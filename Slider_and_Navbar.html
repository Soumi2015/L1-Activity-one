Chinese Cuisine

Chinese cuisine is one of the world's oldest and most diverse culinary traditions. Characterized by profound regional variation and an emphasis on texture, aroma, and color, the diet traditionally balances grains, proteins—notably tofu and pork—and an abundant array of fresh vegetables. Ginger, garlic, and chili are essential building blocks for most savory dishes. Rice is the primary staple in the warm, humid South, while wheat-based products like noodles, steamed buns, and dumplings dominate the colder, drier North. The flavor base is often enriched with soy sauce, rice vinegar, Shaoxing rice wine, sesame oil, and fermented bean pastes. Traditional Chinese cuisine is largely dairy-free. Calcium and protein needs are met instead with dairy alternatives like soy milk and tofu.
